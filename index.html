Cost Accounting Workbook Audit
Full-Year Financial Workbook Audit · Excel & Cost Accounting

The trigger was a mismatch between the number of sales recorded and the balances that should have resulted from them, discovered while reviewing the cost-accounting sheets we use ahead of bookkeeping. The gap traced back further than expected, so I had to go back to January 2023 and correct the figures systematically from there. Along the way, errors surfaced across twelve separate categories, the kind that compound quietly until closing balances stop making sense. I audited the entire workbook month by month, traced each error back to its source, and rebuilt it into a clean, reconciled file with verified closing balances.

🔗
Cross-Sheet References
Traced and repaired broken formula links between monthly sheets that had silently stopped updating.
📉
Cascading Errors
Identified a pricing mistake in one month that had been carrying forward and compounding across several others.
🧾
Missing Source Data
Located and reintegrated purchase records that had been left out of the original entries.
✅
Verified Closing Balances
Reconciled every month so the final balances could be trusted as the following period's opening figures.

The Hard Part

None of the twelve error categories existed in isolation. Several fed into each other across months, so fixing one in place without tracing its downstream effect would have just relocated the mistake. I was still new to accounting at the time, so the audit had to move chronologically, verifying each month was clean before trusting the next.

02

Payroll Reconciliation
Full Calendar-Year Payroll Audit · Payroll & Reconciliation

A full year of payroll needed to be checked across three independent sources (the payroll register, the cash book, and individual payslips) to confirm every employee had been paid correctly and every entry was properly tagged. Discrepancies between the three don't announce themselves; they have to be hunted down line by line.

📋
Three-Way Cross-Check
Matched payroll register, cash book, and payslips against each other for a full calendar year.
🏷️
Untagged Entries
Surfaced cash book entries that had never been properly categorized against payroll.
👤
Per-Employee Rollups
Corrected individual employee totals that had drifted from the source records over time.
📆
Full-Year Coverage
Verified every month, not a sample, so no quarter was assumed clean without being checked.

The Hard Part

Three sources rarely disagree in an obvious way. A mismatch usually shows up as a small rounding-looking gap that's actually a missed entry. Finding those meant reconciling employee by employee, month by month, rather than trusting that totals which looked close enough were actually correct.

The Real Find

While cross-checking cash book entries against current staff, I found a salary payment that didn't match anyone on the active roster. Tracing it back showed an employee who had already left the company was still being paid. Confirming it took about three days of tracing transactions and checking names against employee records, and it's the clearest case for why payroll has to be checked against independent sources rather than trusted on its own.

01
Live & Shipped
SplitZK
Shared Expense Splitter · Web Application

SplitZK exists because "just Mobile Money me back" breaks down the moment three or more people share a bill. It tracks who paid for what (rent, groceries, a trip) and reduces everyone's tangled IOUs down to the fewest possible payments, in Zambian Kwacha, with no spreadsheet required.

Unlike NyumbaNi and CariSave, this one is not a work-in-progress. It is finished and deployed: a deliberately small, fully-shipped tool built to prove I can take something from idea to live product without leaving it half-built.

👥
Multi-Person Splitting
Add anyone in the group and split any expense across exactly who was there.
🧾
Expense Ledger
Every bill logged with who paid, what it was for, and how it was split.
⚖️
Running Balances
See at a glance who is owed money and who owes it, updated instantly.
🔁
Settle-Up Optimizer
A greedy debtor-creditor match collapses every debt into the minimum number of payments.
🇿🇲
ZMW-Native
Amounts formatted in Kwacha from the ground up, not an afterthought retrofit.
⚡
Zero Dependencies
Vanilla HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. No framework, no build step, no bloat.

The Hard Part

Tracking who-paid-what is easy. Reducing a group's tangled debts to the fewest possible payments is not: it's a matching problem. SplitZK sorts everyone into debtors and creditors, then greedily pairs the largest debtor against the largest creditor until every balance clears, so three friends never end up making six payments when two would settle it.

NyumbaNi
Property Listing & Rental Platform · Web Application

NyumbaNi is a direct-to-landlord housing platform designed specifically for the Zambian rental market. The platform eliminates property agents from the equation entirely, allowing tenants to browse listings freely and pay a flat K50 subscription to unlock landlord contact details directly.

In Zambia, the current system forces tenants through layers of agent fees: a K100 non-refundable viewing fee per property visit, and a K1,000+ commission if the tenant proceeds. NyumbaNi removes all of that. One subscription. Unlimited connections. No agents. Every successful contact saves a tenant approximately K1,100.

🏘️
Property Listings
Landlords post directly: photos, prices, locations, and descriptions, fully transparent.
🔍
Search & Filtering
Filter by city, type, price range, and neighbourhood. Built for mobile-first Zambian users.
🔒
K50 Paywall Model
Browse freely. Pay only when ready to contact. One subscription covers all landlords.
📱
Mobile Money Payments
Airtel Money and MTN MoMo integration, the dominant payment methods in Zambia.
🛡️
Trust & Verification
Bronze, Silver, and Gold landlord tiers. Report system. Listings auto-expire after 60 days.
🏠
Landlord Dashboard
Post and manage listings, track tenant enquiries, and upgrade verification tier.

The Hard Part

The paywall logic has to feel fair, not extractive: one payment must unlock every landlord, indefinitely, without turning into a subscription trap. That means designing the access-control state up front, before a single line of the payment integration exists, so the free-browse and paid-contact boundary stays airtight as the listing count grows.

CariSave
Personal Finance App · Android & iOS + Web Platform

CariSave began as an Excel-based financial tracking system, a personal tool built out of necessity. The goal now is to transform that logic into a full mobile application for Android and iOS, supported by a web platform, making personal financial management accessible to everyday Zambians regardless of their income level or smartphone capability.

The target users are students, workers, families, and small business owners: anyone who wants to track income, build saving habits, and make smarter purchasing decisions. CariSave is designed to be lightweight, practical, and usable even on low-end smartphones.

📊
Financial Dashboard
Live overview of income, expenses, savings progress, and monthly summaries in one place.
📝
Expense & Income Tracking
Log transactions by category with full history. Understand exactly where money goes.
🎯
Savings Goals
Set targets, track progress, and receive reminders. Build financial discipline over time.
🛒
Supermarket Price Comparison
Compare product prices across stores. Search items. Make cost-effective decisions every shop.
📈
Reports & Analytics
Monthly and yearly summaries with visual charts. Understand financial patterns over time.
🔔
Smart Notifications
Savings reminders, budget alerts, and price updates to keep users on track effortlessly.
👥
User Contribution System
Users can submit and correct product prices, keeping the comparison data accurate and current.
🔐
Security & Privacy
Secure login, user profiles, cloud storage, and strong data privacy as a foundation, not an afterthought.

The Hard Part

Price comparison data is only useful if it stays accurate, and I don't control what any given supermarket charges tomorrow. The real design problem is the user-contribution and correction system: trust scoring, conflict resolution when two users submit different prices for the same item, and keeping stale entries from misleading someone's grocery budget.
